## Introduction
Duck++ is a programmable desk-side companion inspired by the rubber duck that developers use, except this one actually talks back. 
This series of blog posts will document the development process I've been going through to get this prototype up and running.

I want to explore the following use-cases for this prototype:
- Oration mode - Helps you practise public speaking by measuring filler words (e.g. "umm" or "like"), speaking volume, and overall diction throughout the speech; visualize and suggest areas of improvement.
- Desktop mode - Virtual desktop buddy that appears as a cartoon figure, you can interact with it in various ways (monitor a task and report back when complete)
- Coding mode - helps you reason through problems as you are programming. 
- Note taking mode - Captures your voice to take notes, conditions them (clean up, adds enrichment images, diagrams, etc) and integrates them into obsidian
- Macropad - Quick easy programmable shortcuts  

### Requirements
The device should:
- listen to a person's voice and respond back
- have programmable buttons for various tasks
- display information via an OLED display
- sense when it is picked up and set down 
- is powered from an external battery for portability
- can be programmed/charged/powered via USB-C

### Hardware Design

> Parts List
> - ESP32-S3 - has enough RAM for FreeRTOS + audio buffers
> - ICS-43434 mic + MAX98357A amp (the whole reason this is a rubber duck)
> - SSD1306 OLED (volume, battery %, state feedback)
> - BQ24074 + LiPo + USB-C
> - 2 buttons for boot/reset
> - 4 general purpose buttons
> - LIS2DW12 Accelerometer
> - RGB LED or a couple discrete LEDs for state indication
> - I'll also expose the rest of the free pins on the ESP32 through socket headers for future random bullshit purposes
